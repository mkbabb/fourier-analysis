## §4 — The de-dup (why ~65% of the original I plan converged into glass-ui)

The I charter sequenced 9 waves by **leverage × dependency** with glass-ui as the propagation root. The leverage principle states the verdict plainly: an adoption that lands in `glass-ui/src` propagates to every consumer at once, so glass-ui-rooted waves rank highest. That ranking is also a **de-dup predictor**: the highest-leverage waves are precisely the ones that should NOT be hand-applied per-consumer. So when the constellation's mid-flight resolution folded α (transform-identity, FOUNDATION), β (overlay substrate, KEYSTONE), ζ (top-layer enter/exit), and η (forms/select) into glass-ui's own **AQ** tranche, that was the charter working as designed — not scope-shedding. Those four waves plus the `.deferred-section` utility and the `.scroll-progress`/`view-transition` recipes the δ/ε arms consume are the substrate; fourier's job became **adoption**, not reinvention. What remained genuinely fourier-local was the narrow set the leverage principle leaves behind: the one P0 a11y fix (app-specific markup), the canvas rAF park (app-specific render loop), the θ figure/avatar/CSP leaf (app-specific assets), and the host-side wiring of the glass-ui recipes (the δ progress bar, the ε router bracket, the subpath sweep). That split — ~65% converged, ~35% local — is the leverage principle's signature, made concrete.
